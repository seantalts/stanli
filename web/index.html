stanli: The Stan Language Interpreter - full Stan in the browser

stanc3 (OCaml→JS) compiles the model, stanli.wasm lowers it to an op graph and runs NUTS. No server, no C++ toolchain; everything below happens in this tab. github.com/seantalts/stanli · npm · PyPI

Surprised it was that quick?

There was no compile step. Other Stan interfaces all lower your model to C++ and then use a system C++ compiler to build it, which takes a minimum of 6 seconds for the smallest models even after the precompiled headers have been built. Stanli has its own lowering and optimization passes that run in tens of milliseconds and then just interprets that high level result rather than using a compiler. How it works is the short version, for people who know Stan rather than compilers.

And it is usually faster per gradient, not just to start. Median 2.07x CmdStan across 119 posteriordb models, with 100 of them at or above it. We benchmark every posteriordb model and explain why some are slower: benchmarks. The biggest architectural win is that Stanli analyzes the model graph and pre-allocates a linear array for gradient storage for everything except for ODE UDFs and conditionals on parameters. The other graph optimizations are explained here.

This tab is the slow way to run it, though. The same source compiled to WebAssembly instead of native costs about 1.5x to 2x per gradient: 1.71x geometric mean over seven models. The figures in the paragraph above are native ones, so the margin over CmdStan in here is nearer par than 2x. For work rather than a look, pip install stanli runs the native build.

Numerically accurate and very consistent with CmdStan 118/120 posteriordb models are replayed against the log density, the full gradient and the generated quantities CmdStan actually printed, and 45 agree to the last bit. Worst disagreement anywhere is 2.6e-12 relative. Per-model results: corpus status.
